# Nutrasa — "Start One Level Down" (Education Cut)

**Format:** Educational / explainer video
**Runtime:** 3:00
**Narration:** ~420 words (teaching pace, ~140 wpm)
**Tone:** A good teacher, not a good salesman. Curious, plain-spoken, patient. Explains *why* before it names *what*.
**Voice:** Warm, conversational, second person ("you", "we"). No announcer energy. Music: light, spacious, mostly out of the way — think science documentary, not commercial.
**Visual language:** Animated diagrams, clean 2D motion graphics, macro cell photography, chalk/whiteboard beats. Brand and product do not appear until 2:24.

> **How this differs from the brand cut:** the brand cut sells a company. This one teaches a concept for two and a half minutes and lets the company arrive as the answer. Use this version for social, seminars, training, clinic waiting rooms, and cold audiences.

## THE SCRIPT

### PART 1 — THE LESSON NOBODY GETS TAUGHT (0:00 – 0:30)

**VISUAL:** A person on screen. Camera pushes in past the skin — tissue, then a single glowing cell filling frame. Counter animates up: **30,000,000,000,000**.

**SUPER:** *Your body: 30+ trillion cells.*

> **VO:**
> Here's something most of us are never taught.
> When we talk about health, we talk about the body. Weight. Blood pressure. Energy.
> But the body isn't where health begins. Health begins one level down.
> You are not one thing. You are more than thirty trillion living cells — and every one of them eats, works, repairs itself, and eventually wears out.
> Every symptom you can feel started as something happening in a cell you can't.

---

### PART 2 — WHAT A CELL DOES WHEN IT RUNS SHORT (0:30 – 1:02)

**VISUAL:** Animated cell as a small factory: raw materials arriving, energy being produced, repairs happening. Then supply slows — the animation dims and the factory triages, shutting down side operations one by one. Cut to real life: the 3pm slump, an injury healing slowly, a scale.

**SUPER:** *Energy · Metabolism · Repair — the first things a cell postpones.*

> **VO:**
> Think of a cell as a very small factory. It takes in raw material, makes energy, and repairs itself — every day, without being asked.
> Now here's the important part. When raw material runs short, the cell doesn't shut down. It prioritises.
> It keeps you alive first, and postpones everything else — repair, renewal, energy production.
> That's what poor cellular nutrition may feel like from the outside: low energy, a slower metabolism, recovery that takes longer than it used to.
> And a shortfall that runs for years may quietly add up — contributing to cellular ageing, and a body that doesn't work the way it once did.

---

### PART 3 — HOW THE BODY KNOWS WHERE NUTRIENTS BELONG (1:02 – 1:45)

**VISUAL:** Animation of a protein carrying a glowing tag, being read and routed to the correct compartment — like a parcel with an address moving through a sorting centre. Then archive-texture: 1999 Nobel graphic. Cut to space: a sealed meal pack, an astronaut, Earth through a window.

**SUPER:** *Prof. Günter Blobel — Nobel Prize, 1999* → *NASA: every meal had to be complete.*

> **VO:**
> Which raises a question scientists spent decades on. Inside a cell, how does anything know where to go?
> In 1999, Professor Günter Blobel won the Nobel Prize for the answer. He discovered that our cells know exactly where nutrients belong — material carries what works like an address label, and the cell reads it and routes it to the right place.
> Delivery isn't random. Your body runs a postal system.
> Around the same time, NASA was working the same problem from the other end. In space, there's no supermarket and no second chance — every meal had to deliver complete nutrition, because an astronaut's body had nothing else to draw on.
> Two very different laboratories. One conclusion.
> Give the body the right nutrients, and it has an incredible ability to protect and restore itself.

---

### PART 4 — THE PART MOST PEOPLE MISS (1:45 – 2:24)

**VISUAL:** Side-by-side diagram: a large complex molecule bouncing off a cell wall vs. a small simple one passing through. Then fermentation — traditional jars, miso, yogurt, soy sauce — dissolving into modern stainless steel tanks. Time-lapse counter: **6 months → 1 year → 3 years → 5 years.**

**SUPER:** *What you eat ≠ what you absorb.*

> **VO:**
> And here's the part most people miss. The nutrient you eat is not the nutrient you absorb.
> Large, complex molecules have to be broken down before a cell can take them in — and that work costs the body time and energy it may not have to spare.
> Which is where a very old technology helps. Fermentation.
> Long before laboratories, people learned that microbes could break food down for us, outside the body. It's the principle behind yogurt, soy sauce, miso.
> Modern biotechnology makes that process precise — and gives it time. Ferment a raw material in the right conditions for six months, a year, five years, and much of the digestion is already done before it ever reaches you.
> What arrives is predigested nutrition — in a form the cell is ready to absorb.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

- **The rule that makes this the education cut:** no product name, no logo, and no brand claim before 2:24. If a note gets added that breaks that rule, the video stops teaching and starts selling — and cold audiences drop.
- **Product roll-call is deliberately cut.** Listing twelve products would spend 26 seconds and undo the tone. Put the range on the end card, in the description, or in a follow-up video instead. If a client insists, add after "…rather than the symptom": *"Twelve formulations, from Naturobiotic to Livercare."*
- **Three beats of silence:** after "…a cell you can't" (0:30), after "your body runs a postal system" (1:24), and after "…ready to absorb" (2:24). Each one lets a lesson land.
- **Music discipline:** under 20% volume through Parts 3 and 4. The science sections must sound like information, not persuasion.
- **Diagram budget:** the two animations that earn their cost are the *address-label routing* (Part 3) and *large vs. small molecule at the cell wall* (Part 4). Everything else can be stock or live action.
- **Timing:** reads at 2:54–2:59 cold. If long, trim the yogurt/soy sauce/miso examples to one.
- **Series potential:** each part is a self-contained 30–45 s lesson. Cut as a 4-part social series — *"What your cells do when they run short," "How your body knows where nutrients go," "Why what you eat isn't what you absorb," "What fermentation actually does."* Brand card on each.

## CLAIMS & COMPLIANCE

Same checklist as the brand cut applies. Two points specific to this version:

1. **Blobel's discovery is described, not borrowed.** The line stays at what he actually found — that cells know where material belongs and route it accordingly. It never says his work involved, validated, or endorses Nutrasa. Keep the Nobel graphic and the Nutrasa logo in separate frames.
2. **The fermentation explanation is mechanism, not a health claim.** "Predigested nutrition in a form the cell is ready to absorb" describes what the process does. Adding "100% bioavailable", "clinically proven", or any absorption percentage turns it into a claim requiring study data on file — keep those out of VO unless you hold the substantiation.
