## Posting setup

**Images (carousel order matters):**
1. Lead with the **product box shot** (pink/brown 3D box) — it's clean, high-contrast, and reads on a small screen.
2. Follow with the **9-ingredient grid**, then the individual ingredient cards 01→09 if posting as a carousel or multi-image post.
3. If posting a single image, use the box shot — the grid is too dense to read in-feed and loses the curiosity gap.

**Structure checklist:**
- [ ] Hook lives in the first 125 characters, above "See more"
- [ ] One idea per line, blank line between blocks (wall-of-text kills reach)
- [ ] Emojis as visual bullets only — not decoration in every sentence
- [ ] Exactly one CTA, and make it a *low-effort* one (comment a word > "click link in bio")
- [ ] Link, if any, goes in the **first comment**, not the post body
- [ ] 5–10 hashtags max, at the very bottom

**First-comment template (pin this):**
> Full ingredient list, how to take it, and pricing 👉 [link]
> Questions welcome — I read every comment ☕

**Best time to post:** early morning (6:30–8:30am local) — the post is about a morning ritual, so it lands while people are literally making coffee. Second window: 8–10pm.

**Engagement plan for the first hour:** reply to every comment within 60 minutes. On a "comment a keyword" CTA, reply publicly *and* DM — the public reply is what feeds the algorithm.

## ⚠️ Compliance notes (read before publishing)

This is a dietary supplement, and Facebook/Meta ad policy plus most advertising standards restrict how it can be promoted. To keep the post (and the ad account) safe:

- **Don't promise weight loss, amounts, or timelines** — no "lose 5kg in 15 days," no "burns fat fast," no "guaranteed results."
- **Don't use before/after body photos.** Meta prohibits them outright for health and weight-loss products.
- **Don't address personal attributes** in ad copy — no "Are you overweight?", "Struggling with your belly?" Meta rejects ads that imply knowledge of the viewer's health or body. Write about the product, not about the reader's body.
- **Don't claim it treats, prevents, or cures anything** (diabetes, obesity, constipation as a condition, etc.).
- **Keep the standard disclaimer** where required in your market: *"This product is a dietary supplement and is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease. Results vary. Use alongside a balanced diet and active lifestyle."*
- **Verify every ingredient claim against your actual formulation and dosages** before publishing, and check the label claims permitted by your local regulator (e.g. BPOM, FDA, MOH/HSA) — the ingredient descriptions in the design assets are marketing copy, not approved claims.
- Consider a soft line in the post such as *"Not suitable for pregnant/breastfeeding women or children. Consult your doctor if you have a medical condition."*

The copy above is written to stay descriptive ("supports," "traditionally used for," "helps transport") rather than promissory. If you edit it, keep that verb style.
