## Posting setup

**Images:**
- **Single image → use the product box shot** (pink/brown 3D box). Clean, high-contrast, reads instantly on a phone. This is the one to use.
- Since the post no longer talks about ingredients, **don't lead with the 9-card ingredient grid** — it contradicts the message and is unreadable in-feed anyway. Save those cards for the first comment, a follow-up post, or the product page for people who ask.
- Lifestyle shot (cup in hand, morning light, box in frame) works even better than the box alone if you can shoot one.

**Structure checklist:**
- [ ] Hook lives in the first 125 characters, above "See more"
- [ ] One idea per line, blank line between blocks — walls of text kill reach
- [ ] Emojis as visual bullets only, not decoration in every sentence
- [ ] Exactly one CTA, and make it low-effort (comment a word > "click link in bio")
- [ ] Link, if any, goes in the **first comment**, not the post body
- [ ] 5–10 hashtags max, at the very bottom

**First-comment template (pin this):**

**Best time to post:** 6:30–8:30am local. It's a post about a morning ritual, so it lands while people are literally making coffee. Second window: 8–10pm.

**First hour:** reply to every comment within 60 minutes. On a "comment a keyword" CTA, reply publicly *and* DM — the public reply is what feeds the algorithm.

## ⚠️ Compliance notes (read before publishing)

This is a dietary supplement, so Meta's ad policy and most advertising standards restrict how it can be promoted. To keep the post and the ad account safe:

- **No weight-loss promises, amounts, or timelines** — no "lose 5kg in 15 days," no "burns fat," no "guaranteed results."
- **No before/after body photos.** Meta prohibits them outright for health and weight-loss products.
- **Don't address personal attributes in ad copy** — no "Are you overweight?", "Struggling with your belly?" Meta rejects ads that imply knowledge of the viewer's body. Note that Variant B's "you know that bloated feeling" is a *shared experience* line, not a personal-attribute claim — that's the line to stay on the right side of. If your ad account is already restricted, use Variant A or C for paid.
- **No claims to treat, prevent or cure anything** — constipation, obesity, IBS, diabetes, etc.
- **Keep the disclaimer** where your market requires it: *"This product is a dietary supplement and is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease. Results vary. Use alongside a balanced diet and active lifestyle."*
- Consider adding: *"Not suitable for pregnant or breastfeeding women or children. Consult your doctor if you have a medical condition."*
- **Check what benefit wording your regulator allows** (BPOM / FDA / MOH / HSA depending on market) — "supports healthy metabolism" and "supports digestion" are generally the safe structure-function phrasings, which is why the copy uses them.

All copy above is deliberately descriptive ("supports," "helps") rather than promissory. If you edit it, keep that verb style.
